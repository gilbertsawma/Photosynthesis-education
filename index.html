What is Photosynthesis?

Definition

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy (usually from the sun) into chemical energy stored in glucose (sugar). This energy is then used to fuel the plant's growth and activities.

Key Facts

  • ✓ Occurs primarily in plant leaves
  • ✓ Requires sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide
  • ✓ Produces glucose and oxygen
  • ✓ Foundation of most food chains on Earth
  • ✓ Happens in chloroplasts

Simple Overview

Light Energy + H₂O + CO₂ → Glucose + O₂

The Photosynthesis Process

1

Light Absorption

Chlorophyll in the thylakoid membranes absorbs photons from sunlight, exciting electrons to higher energy states.

2

Water Splitting

Water molecules are split into hydrogen ions, electrons, and oxygen gas. Oxygen is released as a byproduct.

3

Energy Carriers

Energy is captured in ATP and NADPH molecules, which serve as energy carriers for the next stage.

4

Calvin Cycle

ATP and NADPH power the Calvin cycle, which fixes CO₂ into glucose through enzymatic reactions.

Chemical Equations

Overall Photosynthesis Equation

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light Energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Carbon dioxide and water combine to create glucose and oxygen.

Light-Dependent Reactions

2H₂O + 2NADP⁺ + 3ADP + 3Pi → O₂ + 2NADPH + 3ATP

Light energy splits water and creates energy carriers.

Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent)

3CO₂ + 9ATP + 6NADPH → G3P (sugar) + 9ADP + 8Pi + 6NADP⁺

Energy carriers produce glucose from carbon dioxide.
